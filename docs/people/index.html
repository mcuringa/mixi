Josh Chien
BA in Music Education & BA in Music Performance, SUNY Potsdam; MA Educational Technology, Adelphi University

Josh Chien is a doctoral student in the Learning Sciences program at Adelphi University, adjunct professor, and elementary school music teacher. His research involves seeing how strategy and technical execution evolve and improve through discourse in competitive eSports. He is also interested in the learning processes involved in the memorization and execution of Rubik's Cube algorithms with the goal of improving solve times.

Claire D'Emic
BS Biology and BA French, Adelphi University; MS Ecology and Evolution, Fordham University

Claire M. D'Emic is the STEM Initiatives Program Director at the Long Island Children's Museum and a doctoral student in Learning Sciences. Her research interests include cocreating informal science programs with local communities, the role of imagination and curiosity in learning, and the importance of play.

Gabriella Deninno
BA Mathematics, Hofstra University; MSEd Mathematics Education with STEM integration, Hofstra University

Gabriella Deninno is a computer science teacher at Mineola UFSD and a doctoral student in Learning Sciences. Her research interests include embodied learning as it relates to mental representation building in K-12 computer science and mathematics education.

Grace Qi Hu

Ryan Foley

Ryan Foley is the Director of Undergraduate Programs in the Robert B Willumstad School of Business at Adelphi University and a doctoral student in Learning Sciences. Sultana Yasmeen Jamal
BBA Business Studies, American College of Dubai; MBA Human Resource Management, East Delta University

Sultana Yasmeen Jamal is a doctoral student in Learning Sciences at Adelphi University. Erin Puglisi

Nikki Roda

Jennifer Southard
MS English Education, Hofstra University; MA Educational Technology, Adelphi University

Jennifer Southard is the Assistant Director of the Faculty Center for Professional Excellence at Adelphi University, adjunct professor, and Adelphi University PhD candidate. Her research examines how writing is shifting from a static response to an immediate exigency into a distributed, sense-making process among human, technological, and environmental agent, redefining AI as a partner in co-composition to better prepare writers for increasingly complex, post-human communication milieus.
