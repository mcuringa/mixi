Learning Sciences Workshop: Pragmatism, thought, and reason

Fall 2026 · ELS 0815-821-001

Description

This workshop takes inquiry, meaning, and practice as its central concerns. We examine how people make sense of the world through action, how knowledge is built in and through social interaction, and what it means to study these processes as researchers. Readings draw from philosophy, social theory, linguistics, and the anthropology of work and technology, tracing the threads that connect American Pragmatism to symbolic interactionism, ethnomethodology, and the study of situated practice.

The course is also about the practice of inquiry itself. A central assignment is a collaborative research project that the class designs, conducts, and writes together, working toward a manuscript suitable for journal submission by the end of the semester.

Goals & objectives

By the end of the course, you should be able to:

  • Examine how meaning and knowledge are produced through everyday practice and social interaction.
  • Trace key theoretical traditions that have shaped the study of inquiry, meaning, and practice in the learning sciences and related - fields.
  • Situate your own research questions within the frameworks and debates examined in the course.
  • Contribute to a collaborative research process from initial design through analysis and written argument.
  • Produce scholarly work oriented toward peer-reviewed publication in the learning sciences.

Participation

  • Attend all class meetings in person and online.
  • Be prepared to discuss the readings.
  • Collaborate with your peers.
  • Set your own agenda.
  • Have an open mind.
  • Work to improve the workshop for everyone.

Student Led Seminar

You will work with a classmate to select reading(s) for one of our online sessions. You should choose a topic that meets these criteria:

  • is relevant to the course themes and readings
  • is interesting to you and forwards your own research
  • involves some type of experimental research in the learning sciences

Readings should be roughly 30-60 pages in length and should be disseminated two weeks before we meet.

Research Abstract

We are going to find a conference to submit our work too. As a group, we will submit 2 poster proposals. (Teams of 3, plus the instructors.) However, everyone will write a 300 word abstract for a poster so that we can discuss them in class. Your abstract will follow the same format/criteria as the actual conference call for proposals.

You will present your abstract on Oct 22.

Conference Paper

We will follow the call for proposals and write a collaborative paper for submission, following the criteria of the conference. The paper will be written in teams of 3, plus the instructors.

Poster

Even though we won’t know if our papers will be accepted, we will design and present our research at the end of semester STEAM mini-conference at the Manhattan Center. Your team will be evaluated on both the design of your (probably digital) poster and your live presentation of your research.
